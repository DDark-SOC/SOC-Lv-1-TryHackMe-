Report Format<br>

Who: Which user logs in, runs the command, or downloads the file<br>
What: What exact action or event sequence was performed<br>
When: When exactly did the suspicious activity start and ended<br>
Where: Which device, IP, or website was involved in the alert<br>
Why: The most important W, the reasoning for your final verdict

Communication Cases<br>
- You need to escalate an urgent, critical alert, but L2 is unavailable and does not respond for 30 minutes.<br>
Ensure you know where to find emergency contacts. First, try to call L2, then L3, and finally your manager.<br>
- The alert about Slack/Teams account compromise requires you to validate the login with the affected user.<br>
Do not contact the user through the breached chat - use alternative contact methods like a phone call.<br>
- You receive an overwhelming number of alerts during a short period of time, some of which are critical.<br>
Prioritise the alerts according to the workflow, but inform your L2 on shift about the situation.<br>
- After a few days, you realise that you misclassified the alert and likely missed a malicious action.<br>
Immediately reach out to your L2 explaining your concerns. Threat actors can be silent for weeks before impact.<br>
- You can not complete the alert triage since the SIEM logs are not parsed correctly or are not searchable.<br>
Do not skip the alert - investigate what you can and report the issue to your L2 on shift or SOC engineer.<br>
